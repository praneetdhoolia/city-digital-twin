The verdict in one page

The model is well built and honestly documented, and it is not converging on its goal. Those two facts are compatible, and the reason they are compatible is the finding of this pass.

Across five independent review lanes — the code, the documents, the simulator’s own timings, the published field, and the factor ledger — the same defect appeared five times in five different disguises: an instrument or a lever that was not attached to the thing it was believed to measure or control. A count-station comparison pointed at the wrong roads. Four public-transport constants could not reach the router that picks between public-transport modes. A calibration search could reach five of 482 declared fields. Two coefficients that would price service quality reach a JSON file and stop. And mode shares were being tuned against a short-trip deficit that the mobsim structurally cannot reach.

A system cannot be steered through levers that are not connected, and its position cannot be known through instruments pointed somewhere else. That — far more than any behavioural coefficient — is why thirty-one days, sixty arm launches and 405 machine hours have not produced an equilibrium that reproduces the observed city.

The single most consequential finding

The road-counts validation rung has been measuring nothing since the network was rebuilt. count_station_links.csv (16 August) maps each traffic count station to integer MATSim link ids; scenarios/matsim/S2/network.xml.gz was rebuilt on 8 September and renumbers links. 195 of 195 mapped rows now name a different road — 64 point at service roads, 61 at residential streets, 8 at links that no longer exist. Station 55870, “Maitland Road”, points at Talleyrand Circuit, a residential cul-de-sac carrying zero vehicles, while the real Maitland Road in the model carries a median 18,506 vehicles a day against an observed 19,373 — a deviation of −4.5 %, not the −98.7 % the fit reports. Issue #82 and the through-traffic tier built to answer it were responses to a phantom.

The equilibrium question, answered from the run’s own tables

The observed city is not a fixed point of this model. It is a transient the search passes through around iteration 50–100 on its way to somewhere car-heavier.

The board records that running to convergence made the fit worse and asks why. Three things every mode needs, and only two modes have all three

For a simulation to settle organically onto observed reality across many variables at once, every one of those variables needs three properties. Read against the panels above, this model has all three for two of twelve modes.

  1. A channel the search can move it through. Ride sits flat at −38 % to −41 % from iteration 30 onward and never responds to anything; motorbike and truck are locked carves outside the mode-choice set. Public transport reaches only 25.78 % of agents, so three quarters of the population has never held a public-transport plan for any constant to speak to.
  2. A gradient that points at the target. Taxi goes from +88.5 % at the seed to +202.2 % at iteration 300, and light rail from −7.9 % — inside the band — to −57.3 %. For these two the dynamics actively carry the model away from observation. Their best reading in the entire run is the seed, before the simulation has done anything.
  3. A fixed point that is the observed state. Car and walk both have working channels and correct gradients — and both cross their targets around iteration 60 and carry on past. The aggregate objective bottoms at iteration 200 and rises thereafter.

Across thirteen read points of the only run that reached its declared horizon, the count of modes inside 10 % never exceeded two. Every gate reading this project has ever taken was of a model still searching — and the search was passing through a better region than the one it settles in.

Why car wins, and why that one property explains six of the eight failures

The mechanism is visible in a single table. Car’s modelled share is nearly independent of trip distance.

The declared parameters say why. marginalUtilityOfDistance_util_m is 0.0 for every mode; distance is priced only through travel time and, for car, ride and taxi, a monetary rate. Car’s constant is 0.0 while walk’s is +0.35. A one-kilometre car trip therefore costs about two minutes of travel time plus eighteen cents; the same trip on foot costs fifteen minutes. The model charges no fixed per-trip cost for using a car.

Two mechanisms should charge it and do not. routing.accessEgressType is none, so an agent begins and ends a car trip exactly on the activity’s link — no walk to the parked car, at either end. (This is also why public-transport access and egress legs are teleported, issue #167: one config field sits behind both defects.) And parking, though genuinely wired through citysim.ParkingChargeHandler, charges only between 08:00 and 18:00, exempts home activities, and prices only zones above the 90th percentile of job density — 150 of 1,500 core zones. For the great majority of trips the car’s per-trip cost is exactly zero.

As the search proceeds, agents discover this. Car’s share of sub-kilometre trips rises monotonically from 45.4 % at iteration 0 to 63.1 % at iteration 300 — the single most monotone trend in the run — and car, walk, bike and taxi all carry the sign this one mechanism predicts.

This is the distinction the project has been missing, and it reframes the whole calibration effort.

The missing half of the convergence mechanism

One candidate cause of the convergence penalty is a single undeclared field. The run carries fractionOfIterationsToDisableInnovation = 0.8 and fractionOfIterationsToStartScoreMSA = null. The MATSim reference text pairs the two: innovation is switched off and score averaging is started, so that the plan an agent settles onto is chosen on an averaged score rather than on its single most recent noisy execution.

Without it, every agent at the cutoff selects the maximum of noisy estimates at once — which predicts both symptoms this run measured, and no other candidate cause predicts both: the one-iteration snap of +2.211 pp on car, and the average plan score peaking at the cutoff (19.2049) then falling to 14.5679 by iteration 300 as the road everyone chose congests. The field is not declared anywhere in the registry — it is one of the thirty-one undeclared MATSim defaults of issue #155. This is a candidate, not a proven cause; it is simply the cheapest thing to separate.

3. The worked example

Why no check caught it

  • check_manifest.py verifies the file's hash and presence, never that its link ids still resolve in the network it is compared against.
  • _fit.json records station_key, distance and matched_by but NOT the link ids, so the comparison is unauditable from its own output.
  • map_count_stations.py is not re-run by the scenario rebuild; nothing declares the mapping downstream of the network.

The claim, honestly qualified

The empty top rung is partly a reporting convention among agent-based models rather than purely a difficulty. A trip-based four-step model — WFTDM, Wasatch Front — does reach it, reporting six transit modes separately with every mode inside 5 %. It is excluded from the survey for having no agents, which is correct, but the qualification is real and this report states it rather than claiming a clean field.

What the comparison says

  • Nobody in transport treats “all N targets inside their bands” as the object being solved. Across 50 projects the mechanisms actually in use are a weighted sum of normalised deviations, a log-ratio constant update applied mode by mode, and a human deciding when to stop.
  • The profession has written down that it is not normally attempted. The ActivitySim framework, verbatim: “It is typically the case that not all calibration and validation targets can be achieved simultaneously. Therefore, model calibration and validation requires judgement on when the models are reasonably calibrated.”
  • The failure mode has a published name. Chen, Prakash, Lima De Azevedo & Ben-Akiva, Transportation Research Part C 119 (2020) 102650: existing calibration approaches “are myopic heuristics in the sense that they do not consider the fundamental inter-dependencies among choice-models and do not have a systematic way to adjust model parameters” — against a target set that is exactly this project’s: activity shares, mode shares, time-dependent and mode-specific OD flows, and sensor counts.
  • The architecture that solves it uses a MAXIMUM, not a sum. History matching computes an implausibility statistic per output and takes “the maximum implausibility measure across Q outputs”, with model discrepancy as an explicit denominator term. A weighted scalar lets one mode pay for another; a maximum does not. A worked policy-ABM application ruled out >99 % of parameter space in four waves, adding outputs wave by wave.
  • Our convergence penalty has a published diagnosis. Flötteröd and Kickhöfer, MATSim book chapter 49: “When a policy study’s synthetic choice sets are very different from the alternatives considered in the real world, it is unlikely that the simulation will display correct aggregated quantities.” Our final public-transport choice-set coverage is 25.78 %.
  • The counts deficit is not the sample. A 2025 study of a 100 % scenario finds averages “become unreliable when sampling below 10 %”; 25 % is inside the reliable band. Four differently phrased searches found no published case of an agent-based model matching shares while missing volumes by ~99 % — which is itself corroboration that ours was an artefact.
  • There is no published acceptance bar for mode choice at all. “There are no applicable criteria guidelines for checks of mode choice”, nor numeric tolerances for RMSE, GEH, screenlines or trip-length distributions. The 10 % band is self-imposed — which makes it an admirable standard, not a failed one.
  • Somebody has paid for a large simultaneous calibration and published the price. Tallinn/SimMobility: 477 parameters against three equally weighted components, Bayesian optimisation over a random-forest surrogate, satisfactory at iteration 150–182 — roughly 1,000 machine hours.
