Method

This report is a reading of commit bdd40f3 on the session branch, taken on 7 September 2026 while the first family-F27 arm was running (so nothing was recompiled and no matsim.log was read whole). Three collector scripts under .claude/skills/project-report/scripts/ produced the mechanical half — metrics.json (inventory, commit log, growth series, every PR, issue and CI run, the run index, the timeline), code_metrics.json (per-file metrics, long and complex functions, duplicate windows, dead definitions, nested loops, row-wise pandas, wall-clock use, unused imports, swallowed exceptions, literal mode lists, the Java markers and test coverage by module) and performance.json (every run's own stopwatch.csv, memory, telemetry, dumps, config and store).

Fourteen readers then worked in parallel, none of them editing anything: six code reviewers, one per area, each reading every file in the area completely and returning findings with file:line and a failure scenario, an optimisation ledger and five evidenced ratings; one performance analyst over the timing inventory, the Java listeners and the run overlays; one historian over every PR body, the commit log and the timeline; one document and controls reviewer over every living and archived document, hook, workflow, skill and settings file with a relative-link check; three field researchers and three factor researchers working from scratch with web search and fetch only, every cell read this pass, with a stopping rule of three empty rounds.

The synthesis deduplicated findings across reviewers, keeping the sharper citation, re-read a sample of cited lines and re-fetched a sample of research sources before they went in, and diffed everything against the 3 September report's findings table (that report carries no embedded data block, so the diff is by hand on its findings, goal table and recommendations).

The rating rubric

Each area is rated 1–5 on correctness (does it compute what it claims), robustness (what happens under a plausible bad input, a race or a missing file), maintainability (function length, duplication, tests, docstrings), efficiency (repeated parses, nested loops over the population, log reads that scale with the log) and portability (does the framework half read identically for a city it has never seen). A cell carries its two strongest pieces of evidence; ratings are never averaged across areas.

What the mobsim costs

Host 24 cores; qsim.numberOfThreads 16 since HEAD (10 on every arm before), events at 4 threads with synchronizeOnSimSteps, replanning and routing at 20. Thread scaling is flat: 2.4× the qsim threads bought 8 % of the mobsim phase (142 → 131 s); 10 → 16 bought 3-5 s of a 267 s iteration. PassingQ costs ~42 s an iteration over FIFO (§9.59) and stays for correctness. The whole timetable runs at every fraction (sample_population.py:232-244 scales seats only), which is the fixed floor behind the sub-linear 1 % → 10 % pace; the collector's 30 / 33 / 263 s medians pool families F12, F1 and F1-F26 and are not one curve — within a family the scaling is close to linear in agents (F1: 33.3 → 90.2 s for 2.5× the persons).

What replanning costs

Weights 0.7 / 0.15 / 0.1 / 0.05 (ChangeExpBeta, ReRoute, SubtourModeChoice, TimeAllocationMutator), plan memory 8 (sweep [3, 10]), innovation off at 0.8 → iteration 240 of 300. What writing costs

At F26 a milestone directory was 1.8-1.9 GB (0.59 GB plans, 1.11 GB events, 0.17 GB experienced plans) and cost 250-370 s over a plain iteration every tenth iteration — about 36 s an iteration on average and 21.9 GB per 100 iterations. HEAD moves plans and events to every 100 and adds a trips table every 10, so a 300-iteration arm writes about 11 GB and 20 minutes of dumps — and, as the findings say, iterations 10-90 can no longer be read. -Xms40g -Xmx40g -XX:+UseParallelGC and nothing else: no GC log, no ParallelGCThreads. Four independent full decodes of the 1.11 GB events file (close-out accounting, truck stations, replay, and the experienced-plans pass the boardings reader makes), each "minutes"; the gate reporter re-reads B1 (1.3 s), the schedule three times and the plans twice (16.4 s) per iteration under --trend; extract_metrics makes six passes over two tables. Unrecorded. What the comparison says

  • On the validation ladder this project sits alone on the top rung by *design* — twelve per-mode targets, each read at every gate — and at the bottom by *result*: no mode inside 10 % at a gate since bus on 1 Sep. Every other project re-found this pass publishes a fit; this one publishes the distance to one.
  • Of the 40 projects re-found, 23 stop at survey shares plus road link counts (every public MATSim scenario, AToM, eqasim Île-de-France, Tyne and Wear); five reach transit ridership counts (CMAP, PSRC SoundCast, POLARIS Chicago, BEAM Bay Area, MATSim-NYC); none claims per-mode ridership for every simulated mode.
  • The ten LLM-agent simulators of 2023-2026 validate on trajectory statistics or survey shares; the one that closes to a physical multimodal engine (GTA, Berlin, SUMO + OpenTripPlanner) reports a modal-split RMSE of 4.07 percentage points against MiD 2017 and four count stations. None reaches transit ridership.
  • No agent-based model of Newcastle NSW or the Hunter was found in ten search rounds; the nearest Australian analogues are AToM (Melbourne, open, 10 %, mode share and road volumes) and the Greater Bendigo demand build.
  • Every MATSim scenario that publishes a calibration target calibrates to survey shares and counts with ASCs (Cadyts for Vienna and Gauteng); this project refuses compensating constants by rule (GOAL.md loop step 3) and holds six ASCs held_fixed, which is why its deviations are larger and its inputs more numerous (470 declared fields).
  • The reproducible-pipeline column is sparse across the field: the German MATSim scenarios and eqasim publish end-to-end pipelines; the US activity-based models publish calibration reports, not pipelines. This project's manifest-hashed package with a producer per artefact is at the reproducible end, with the defects listed.
  • Physical active modes, physical PT, signals, freight and fares together appear in no other re-found row; the closest are BEAM (PT physical, pricing) and SimMobility (signals, freight). The breadth is the project's distinguishing claim and the source of its calibration burden.
  • Status: 24 of 40 rows are active (a release or paper in 2025-2026); the SimMobility, TRANSIMS, Gauteng, Vienna and several MATSim city rows are dormant. The field is consolidating on MATSim, ActivitySim and POLARIS.

What each does best that we lack

  • A published fit with a metric (every row above): the board states deviations, not a fit statistic; the calibration report covers a 21 Aug base four families old.
  • ASC-free calibration evidence: Vienna and Gauteng show Cadyts count-matching; this project needs the equivalent for a no-compensating-constant loop — a published sensitivity of each deviation to the declared sweeps (245 of 261 never run).
  • Transit ridership by line or station as a scored target with a metric (CMAP, PSRC, POLARIS, BEAM, MATSim-NYC): this project has the disclosed boardings (§9.130) but scores them as a gate flag, not a fit.
  • Run-to-run replication across seeds as a published band (MATSim Open Berlin's calibration notes; the project's own memory says three runs of one build disagree): no arm has been replicated.

  • A population-synthesis validation (eqasim's marginal fit against the HTS; SANDAG's re-estimation on a 2022 survey): the synthetic population is checked by counts, not by a published marginal fit.
  • An LLM-agent activity generator validated against a survey (GTA, MobAgent): not attempted, and not obviously wanted; listed because the field is moving there.
  • A licence: every open row carries one; the repository root has none.
